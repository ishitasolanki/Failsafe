It knows when not to retry.

Failsafe — an autonomous payment-failure recovery agent for Razorpay merchants.

60 held-out payments measured against a baseline reproducible with zero API keys

Payments don't fail for one reason.

They fail for six — and each one needs a different response, at a different time. A single blanket retry gets five of them wrong.

The same retry, six different outcomes

No money in the account Retry on the 1st, when salary lands
Bank was down Retry in ~45 minutes
Card expired Ask them to update it — retrying fails forever
3DS abandoned Send a UPI link instead
Mandate revoked Retrying is a compliance violation
Suspected fraud Retrying invites a chargeback

What broke

Nine compliance violations — from a policy that refuses them.

The policy was fine. A fraud case misdiagnosed as insufficient funds reaches the policy layer already wearing the wrong label, so the terminal check had nothing to fire on.

Safety cannot sit downstream of a model's opinion. Two guards now ignore the diagnosis entirely.

What broke

9 → 0

Violations, after a failure-burst guard and a mandate lookup that never asks the model's opinion.

And once, my agent recovered less money than the dumb baseline. I only caught it because the report prints the baseline next to my own number.

What is real, and what is not

The payments are synthetic. The comparison is still fair.

Razorpay's test mode cannot make a customer pay, so outcomes come from a pre-rolled world — but both strategies face the identical one, and the safety results do not depend on it at all.
